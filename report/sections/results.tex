\subsection{Jev gate}
\label{sec:jev-results}
% Owner: Jalil Jabbarli.
As a first check of the gate, we scored a balanced sample of 10 validation
turns (5 positive, 5 negative, spread across categories with a fixed seed)
with the pinned Jev model. Each turn was scored once; Table~\ref{tab:jev-n10}
applies several thresholds to those saved scores, so the sweep needed no
extra API calls. This sample is far too small to choose $\tau$ and is not
representative of the class balance of the full validation set; we report it
only to show that the gate runs end to end and to identify early failure modes.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Jev gate on a balanced 10-turn validation sample (5 positive,
  5 negative). $K$ is the number of turns sent to the extractor and $S=1-K/N$
  the fraction of extractor calls saved.}
  \label{tab:jev-n10}
  \begin{tabular}{rrrrrrr}
    \toprule
    $\tau$ & $K$ & TP & FP & Recall & Precision & $S$ \\
    \midrule
    0.05 & 8 & 5 & 3 & 1.00 & 0.63 & 0.20 \\
    0.10 & 4 & 3 & 1 & 0.60 & 0.75 & 0.60 \\
    0.30 & 3 & 3 & 0 & 0.60 & 1.00 & 0.70 \\
    0.50 & 2 & 2 & 0 & 0.40 & 1.00 & 0.80 \\
    0.90 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0.20 & 1.00 & 0.90 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Ranking.} The AUROC on this sample is 0.92. All five negatives,
including three keyword-flagged turns chosen to look like constraints, scored
at most 0.11. The two injected constraints scored 0.99 (``never include my
name in your replies'') and 0.86 (a preference for arXiv papers), and a
natural WildChat turn with writing-style rules scored 0.36.

\paragraph{Failure mode.} The two SWE-Natural positives scored only 0.08 and
0.07, below the highest-scoring negative. Both are long GitHub issues
(about 4,000 and 4,800 characters) in which the constraint is a small part of
a task description. They are the only positives missed at $\tau=0.10$
and $\tau=0.30$. As a result, the only threshold on our grid that
reaches the 0.95 recall target on this sample is $\tau=0.05$, which saves just
20\% of extractor calls. Improving recall on long, task-heavy turns without
admitting more negatives is therefore the main question for the full run.

\paragraph{Gate cost and latency.} Scoring all 10 turns cost
\$0.00043 in total (about \$0.000043 per turn, as reported by OpenRouter) at
an average of 1,028 input tokens per call. Mean latency was 0.28\,s per turn
(median 0.25\,s, maximum 0.61\,s). Every gate call succeeded; failed calls are
raised as errors rather than counted as negatives.

\paragraph{Next steps.} We will score all 100 validation turns, select $\tau$
as the highest threshold whose validation recall is at least 0.95, and then report recall on the test
set and calls saved, cost, and latency on the stream set. We also plan to
compare the gate's per-turn cost against logged extractor costs to check the
break-even condition $c_E>c_G/S$ from our cost model.
